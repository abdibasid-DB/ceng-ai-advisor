# Source Collection Batch 04

## Stage

Source Collection - Batch 4: Student Success, GPA, Projects, Internships, Career Progression, Specialization Choice, and Common Misconceptions

## Project

AI-Powered Computer Engineering Student Advisor

## Date Accessed

2026-08-30

## Scope

This batch strengthens evidence for student success questions that appeared in the example-question scope: GPA versus projects, low GPA concerns, internships, practical experience, career progression, beginner and late-start concerns, specialization choice, graduate studies, and common misconceptions about Computer Engineering and computing.

No preprocessing, chunking, embeddings, ChromaDB, Ollama, RAG pipeline, chatbot logic, frontend features, backend features, or application functionality were implemented.

## Collection Rules Applied

- Accepted only real and verifiable sources.
- Prioritized professional organizations, employer surveys, university career centers, university academic advising pages, and computing education organizations.
- Did not invent career timelines, GPA rules, internship outcomes, specialization rankings, or gender claims.
- Used `null` where exact publication dates were not visible.
- Recorded limitations when evidence was general, U.S.-focused, institution-specific, or not Computer Engineering-only.

## GPA and Projects Evidence Added

- NACE 2025 employer evidence about declining GPA screening and skills-based hiring.
- MIT portfolio guidance about demonstrating skills, accomplishments, projects, and professional identity.
- Berkeley resume guidance about when to include GPA and how to show projects, work, research, volunteer experience, and transferable skills.

Important limitation: The evidence does not support a universal claim that projects always matter more than GPA. It supports a more careful answer: GPA can matter in some contexts, but employers and career centers also emphasize skills, experience, and evidence of work.

## Internship Evidence Added

- NACE 2024 employer evidence about internship hiring, sourcing, selection, competencies, previous internship experience, career fairs, and hybrid internship formats.
- NACE 2026 evidence about experiential learning, internships, hackathons, and examples of skill development.

Important limitation: The evidence is mostly U.S.-based and does not prove that a student without internship experience cannot find work.

## Career Progression Evidence Added

- SFIA programming/software-development levels describe increasing responsibility from assisted work through complex work, technical responsibility, and leadership.
- NACE career-readiness evidence supports continual development of communication, teamwork, professionalism, critical thinking, and skill demonstration.

Important limitation: No accepted source gives a universal number of years required to become senior. The future advisor should avoid fixed timelines unless quoting a specific source-specific observation.

## Beginner and Late-Start Evidence Added

- Brown CS advising evidence shows that multiple introductory computing pathways can be designed for students without prior programming background.
- This supports careful responses to "Is it too late to start programming at university?" without claiming that all programs are equally beginner-friendly.

## Specialization-Choice Evidence Added

- University of Illinois ECE subdiscipline evidence maps Computer Engineering areas such as hardware systems, cyberphysical systems, security, networking, mobile/distributed computing, big data, AI, robotics, and cybernetics.
- ACM CCECC guidance helps distinguish Computer Engineering from other computing disciplines.

Important limitation: These sources do not rank one specialization as best. Specialization advice should depend on student interests, strengths, course exposure, projects, internships, and market evidence.

## Graduate-Study Evidence Added

- MIT graduate/professional school advising explains when graduate study may help, what questions students should ask, and what preparation matters.
- The source supports balanced advice about graduate school versus entering the workforce.

Important limitation: It does not say every Computer Engineering student needs a master's degree.

## Common-Misconception Evidence Added

- NCWIT Scorecard supports gender-participation and workforce questions with data.
- NCWIT engagement guidance directly addresses overly narrow ideas about computing work and who belongs in computing.
- ACM CCECC guidance supports the point that Computer Engineering includes hardware, software, and communications, so it is not only software or only hardware.
- Brown CS advising supports the point that prior programming experience is not always required to begin computing study.
